# Overview Hydraulic Parameters

## 1. No freezing

### 1.1 Water retention curve $\theta(h_c)$

van Genuchten, with the capillary head $h_c\geq0$ (suction) and $m = 1-\frac{1}{n}$:

$$
\theta=\theta_r+(\theta_s-\theta_r)\left(1+(\alpha h_c)^n\right)^{\frac{1-n}{n}}
$$

The effective saturation is defined as

$$
S_e=\frac{\theta-\theta_r}{\theta_s-\theta_r}.
$$

### 1.2 Relative hydraulic conductivity $k_{rel}$

van Genuchten-Mualem:

$$
k_{rel}(S_e)=\sqrt{S_e}\left(1-\left(1-S_e^\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^2
$$

## 2. With freezing

In the frozen soil, the total water content $\theta(h_c)$ from the water retention curve is split into a liquid part $\theta_l$ and an ice part $\theta_i=\theta-\theta_l$, and the relative hydraulic conductivity becomes a function of capillary head and temperature, $k_{rel}(h_c,T)$. The density difference between ice and liquid water is neglected.

Five parametrizations are compared (see the table in the README for the names used in the code):

| Label | Parametrization | Phase partitioning | $k_{rel}$ |
|-------|-----------------|--------------------|-----------|
| (A) | drying assumption | SFC (PC) | $k_{rel}(S_{e,l})$ |
| (B) | drying assumption with impedance factor, $\Omega=10$ | SFC (PC) | $I(\theta_i)\,k_{rel}(S_{e,l})$ |
| (C) | impedance factor, $\Omega=10$ | SFC (PC) | $I(\theta_i)\,k_{rel}(S_e)$ |
| (D) | impedance factor, $\Omega=50$ | SFC (PC) | $I(\theta_i)\,k_{rel}(S_e)$ |
| (E) | capillary bundle model | Gibbs-Thomson | Watanabe & Flury [2] |

### 2.1 Soil Freezing Curve (SFC)

<!-- DRAFT: T_f and omega taken from petrophysic.phase_separation, please check -->

If $T$ is greater than the freezing temperature $T_f$, all water is liquid ($\theta_l=\theta$). Otherwise

$$
\theta_l=\theta_r+(\theta-\theta_r)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{T-T_f}{\omega}\right)^2\right) \tag{PC}
$$

$$
\theta_i=\theta-\theta_l
$$

with $T_f = 0\,°C$ and $\omega = 1\,°C$. This empirical freezing curve is also implemented in permaFoam [1].

<!-- END DRAFT -->

**Remark (Clausius-Clapeyron equation):** the phase-change equation (PC) is an empirical parametrization, which is not based on the Clausius-Clapeyron equation. The water content could be related to temperature with the Clausius-Clapeyron equation, which relates temperature to pressure under the assumption of thermal equilibrium. When neglecting the deformation of the soil due to freezing/thawing, the equation is given by

$$
\frac{\partial P_w}{\partial T}\approx \frac{H_f\rho_l}{T}
$$

with $T$ in Kelvin. Integrating with respect to $T$ from the melting temperature $T_m$ results in

$$
P_w = H_f\rho_l \ln\left(\frac{T}{T_m}\right)\Leftrightarrow h_w=\frac{H_f}{g}\ln\left(\frac{T}{T_m}\right).
$$

This relation is not used in the parametrizations (A)-(D). The capillary bundle model (E) uses the related Gibbs-Thomson equation (see 2.4).

### 2.2 Drying assumption

<!-- DRAFT: written from parameter_interpolation.interpolate_parameter_drying, please check -->

The drying assumption treats ice like air in the van Genuchten-Mualem model: freezing is assumed to have the same effect on the flow of liquid water as drying. The liquid water content is calculated with the SFC (PC), and the relative hydraulic conductivity is calculated from the effective saturation of the liquid water only:

$$
S_{e,l}=\frac{\theta_l-\theta_r}{\theta_s-\theta_r}, \qquad k_{rel}=k_{rel}(S_{e,l}).
$$

**(B) Drying assumption with impedance factor:** in addition, the relative hydraulic conductivity is reduced with the impedance factor (2.3) with $\Omega=10$:

$$
k_{rel}=I(\theta_i)\,k_{rel}(S_{e,l}).
$$

<!-- END DRAFT -->

### 2.3 Impedance factor (permaFoam [1])

<!-- DRAFT: written from parameter_interpolation.interpolate_parameter_imp10/imp50, please check -->

The relative hydraulic conductivity is calculated from the effective saturation of the total water content $S_e$ (i.e. as if the soil was unfrozen) and reduced with the impedance factor

$$
I(\theta_i)=\max\left(10^{-\Omega\theta_i},\,k_{f,min}\right), \qquad k_{rel}=I(\theta_i)\,k_{rel}(S_e)
$$

with $k_{f,min}=10^{-6}$ and $\Omega=10$ (C) or $\Omega=50$ (D). The liquid and ice contents are calculated with the SFC (PC).

<!-- END DRAFT -->

### 2.4 Capillary Bundle Model [2]

<!-- DRAFT: written from petrophysics_capillaryBundles.py and interpolate_parameter_cap, please check -->

The pore space is represented as a bundle of cylindrical capillaries. Each effective saturation $S_e$ corresponds to the pore radius that is just filled with water at that saturation (Young-Laplace equation with the van Genuchten retention curve):

$$
R(S_e)=\frac{2\sigma_{aw}\alpha}{\rho_l g}\left(S_e^{-\frac{n}{n-1}}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{n}}
$$

with the air-water surface tension $\sigma_{aw}=0.0756\,N\,m^{-1}$.

**Ice formation (Gibbs-Thomson):** below the melting temperature $T_m$, ice forms in pores larger than the critical radius

$$
r_{GT}(T)=\frac{\sigma_{ls}}{\rho_i H_f}\frac{T_m}{T_m-T}
$$

($\sigma_{ls}=0.029\,N\,m^{-1}$, $T$ in Kelvin). A liquid film of thickness

$$
d(T)=\left(\frac{-A}{6\pi\rho_i H_f}\frac{T_m}{T_m-T}\right)^{1/3}
$$

remains between the ice and the pore wall ($A=-10^{-19.5}\,J$ is the Hamaker constant). In a water-filled pore with radius $R$, the radius of the ice core is

$$
r_i = R - d \quad \text{if } R-d>r_{GT}, \quad \text{otherwise } r_i=0.
$$

**Liquid and ice contents:** integrating over all water-filled pores gives the liquid and ice saturations

$$
S_{e,l}(S_e,T)=\int_0^{S_e}\left(1-\frac{r_i^2}{R^2}\right)dS, \qquad S_{e,i}(S_e,T)=\int_0^{S_e}\frac{r_i^2}{R^2}\,dS,
$$

and $\theta_l=\theta_r+(\theta_s-\theta_r)S_{e,l}$.

**Relative hydraulic conductivity:** the flow in a pore with an ice core is reduced by the factor

$$
f_q=\sqrt{1-\frac{r_i^4}{R^4}+\frac{\left(1-\frac{r_i^2}{R^2}\right)^2}{\ln\left(\frac{r_i}{R}\right)}}
$$

($f_q=1$ for pores without ice). With Mualem's model, the relative hydraulic conductivity is

$$
k_{rel}(S_e,T)=\sqrt{S_{e,l}}\left(\frac{\int_0^{S_e}R(S)\,f_q(S,T)\,dS}{\int_0^{1}R(S)\,dS}\right)^2.
$$

### 2.5 Lookup tables

<!-- DRAFT: written from parameter_interpolation.py, please check -->

Evaluating the parametrizations (in particular the integrals of the capillary bundle model) during the simulation would be too expensive. Therefore $\theta_l$ and $k_{rel}$ are precomputed once for each soil type on a grid of capillary heads $h_c$ (from $S_e\in(0,1)$) and temperatures $T$, and stored as 2D interpolators (`interpax.Interpolator2D`, monotonic interpolation). These lookup tables are saved under `input/interpolation/` and evaluated in each Newton-Raphson iteration. Since the interpolators are differentiable, the Jacobian can still be computed by automatic differentiation.
